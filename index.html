02 THE MAY THESIS

Scale tells you where the ceiling is

In April we validated a funnel. In May we scaled it on purpose — because the only way to learn where a funnel breaks is to push it until it does.

The April rule is unchanged and auditable: concentrate finite cash on the bets that return fastest, and keep every deferral on a clock. May added a second principle on top. Once a model is validated, scaling it is itself a measurement. We raised spend across three sequential launches and read the same funnel under three different loads — warm audience, medium scale, and full scale with operational adversity.

Three facts came out of that measurement. The Traidon ticket held at exactly US$ 251 across all three launches — the product is priced right. The bottleneck is the front-end cost of acquiring cold leads — front CPA, not the offer. And when a separate bet, the US Claude Course test, failed its hypothesis, we cut it at US$ 3,700 rather than feed it. Built to scale on what works; disciplined enough to stop what does not.

05 TRAIDON · DISTRIBUTED BY BITGET

Three launches, one fixed price

In May, TrAIdon went from listed to launched — three paid rounds through the 20 Setups funnel, on our own network.

It is a web application that analyses the trader's operations in real time against a current set of forty strategies, returning tactical reads without requiring the user to assemble the analysis themselves. The model is the one the group began with 2K and M4 Markets: a proprietary platform built from day one with a distribution partner in mind.

Across the three May launches the subscription held at exactly US$ 251, selling 41 subscriptions on 654 front-end tickets, for US$ 19,051 in revenue — all through our own network. TrAIdon is also listed on Bitget; that commercial arrangement is contracted, with the first invoice expected in June. It adds a channel alongside our own; it is not a partnership through which Bitget opens markets for us. The decisive read: the product holds its price even as audiences cool. The lever to work now is acquisition cost, not the offer.

06 DESAFIO 20 SETUPS · SCALED

The funnel we scaled until it showed its ceiling

The funnel that paid for itself in April was put under load in May. Three launches, rising budget, and one number that explains the whole story: front CPA.

Across three launches we lifted spend from US$ 2,772 to US$ 5,166. Revenue and conversion fell as we bought colder traffic — yet the Traidon ticket never moved off US$ 251, and the front-end order bumps actually tripled (8 → 21). The number that tells the story is the front CPA: US$ 10.42 → US$ 18.45 → US$ 34.21. With a fixed ticket and ~5% back-end conversion, the ROAS slide is a mathematical consequence of acquisition cost. The funnel still closed positive — US$ 6,741 in contribution at a 1.55× operating ROAS (Traidon funnel only). Including the US$ 3,700 US Claude Course test we cut (§03), all-in May spend was US$ 16,010 and aggregate ROAS 1.19× — still cash-positive, and cutting the test early is why the gap stays small. Front CPA — not the product — is the metric June attacks, with new creative and a second network.

10 SEQUENCING

What we deferred and why

To sequence is to decide, on a clock, in what order each bet receives people and cash. In May we cut one bet outright, held two on the clock, and carried April's deferrals forward — each still on a dated trigger.

10.1 ◐ CLAUDE COURSE · US ENTRY · tested and cut

We tested entry into the US market with direct sales of the Claude Course ticket on LinkedIn and Meta. US$ 3,700 in, zero sales — and we shut the funnel down. This was a clear hypothesis with a defined stop criterion, not a blind attempt: a controlled US$ 3,700 ceiling against the US$ 12,310 deployed on the Traidon launches in Brazil. The learning is already incorporated — 'build the base before selling' is now a prerequisite for any new-market entry, starting with the Health niche.

Re-engagement trigger: a base built first — we re-enter a new market only after audience and pain are validated.

10.2 ◐ 2K · USER ACQUISITION TOOL · backlog, no defined timeline

The 2K still captures well, but the structural issues identified earlier — the download-to-revenue bottleneck and elevated maintenance cost — are unchanged. With May's capital concentrated on the Traidon launches, the 2K received no structural investment and has no defined timeline. The planned repair waits on Traidon cash and the front-CPA learnings now in hand. The tool stays live and capturing; it simply is not first in line.

Re-engagement trigger: Traidon cash stabilized + the front-CPA fix proven on the live funnel.

10.3 ◐ GLOBAL DIRECT SALES · rescheduled in the calendar

The first read on price, ticket and customer profile outside Brazil was rescheduled. The reasoning is sequencing: the same paid-media muscle returns more, sooner, by first duplicating the proven Traidon funnel onto João Homem's network — a second domestic distribution channel — and is carried abroad once that network performs. The US Claude Course test (10.1) is itself a global-sales lesson already banked.

Re-engagement trigger: João Homem's network funnel live and performing.

10.4 ◐ AUGUR · NELOGICA · IB DEALS · carried over, triggers unchanged

Three April deferrals had no new movement in May — by focus, not neglect. Augur / prediction markets: the Nexus Engine is built and still waits on regulatory clarity or an international monetization window. Nelogica: the contract is still in their legal team's queue — friction is legal, not commercial. IB Deals: Tickmill and VCG stay active and yielding daily alongside M4, Juno and Kucoin; the remaining brokers stay queued behind a clean 2K funnel.

Re-engagement trigger: as stated in April — each front keeps its original dated trigger.
